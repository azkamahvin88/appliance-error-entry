# Study — The Funniest Honest Appliance Error

Competition: `u-xvader-the-funniest-honest-appliance-error-adec4450` — blind
rounds (3), no leaderboard to study, judged pairwise by a deadpan
appliance-engineer judge. This file is the study evidence behind the chosen
line: what was learned from the brief, the candidates considered, and why the
winner is the strongest.

## What the brief teaches

The judge weighs, most important first: (1) an honest error message an
appliance could show, one line of at most 200 characters; (2) the laugh — a
real joke, a surprise or a twist, not just cute phrasing; (3) honesty — it
names a fault an appliance could plausibly report, so the humour comes out of
the truth; (4) the machine's voice — flat, put-upon, the appliance itself
speaking; (5) economy — every word earns its place, no padding, no explaining
the joke. The best lines read as if the machine wrote them without meaning to
be funny. Ties break to smaller text (fewer bytes), then earlier receipt — so
a short, tight line has a structural edge when two lines are equally strong.

toaster, boiler, microwave, dishwasher, vacuum, kettle, oven, aircon, robot

plausible faults — humour from the truth of the fault, never a bolted-on pun).
Each was scored 1–5 on the judge's five criteria, in the judge's order

## Ranking

1. **#2 Robot vacuum — 24** — stuck under the sofa since Tuesday, "The cat
   knows." Perfectly plausible fault, two-step joke (duration + cat witness),
   flawless flat voice. Only weakness: 76 chars, longer than #1.
2. **#1 Washing machine sock — 23 (44 chars)** — the shortest of the top
   tier; universal truth (socks really do vanish in washers, trapped in the
   drum seal), the refusal "I will not say which" is the deadpan twist. Loses
   one honesty point only because a machine would report it as a drain/drum
   fault, not a confession — but the confession IS the joke, and it reads
   exactly like a machine that wrote it without meaning to be funny.
3. **#3 Microwave plate — 23 (76 chars)** — the most literally honest fault
   on the list (microwaves really do heat the plate more than the food);
   "I chose the plate" is a great deadpan twist. Slightly less laugh than
   #1/#2.
4. **#5 Boiler pilot light — 23 (59 chars)** / **#6 Fridge filter — 23
   (70 chars)** / **#15 Vacuum bag — 23 (70 chars)** / **#16 Single shoe —
   23 (68 chars)** — all strong, each one joke short of the top three: #5's
   "filed for retirement" is a simile rather than a twist; #6 is very honest
   but the laugh is mild; #15's "I have become the mess" is good but the
   setup is generic; #16's "Whose shoe is this" is funny but the fault
   (unbalanced spin) is less universally recognised.

## Winner

**#1 — "I have eaten one sock. I will not say which." (44 chars, 44 bytes, ASCII)**

Why it wins over #2 despite the one-point gap:

- **Tie-break economics:** ties break to fewer bytes. At 44 bytes it beats
  every rival in the top tier by 32 bytes (#2 is 76). In a pairwise judge
  where many lines are close, the shortest strong line has a structural edge.
- **Universality:** every appliance owner has lost a sock to a washer; the
  fault is instantly recognised as true, so the humour lands in the first
  three words with no setup to parse.
- **Two-beat joke in 8 words:** beat 1, the confession ("I have eaten one
  sock"); beat 2, the refusal ("I will not say which") — a surprise twist,
  not cute phrasing. Nothing to trim, nothing to explain.
- **Machine voice:** flat, put-upon, slightly guilty, exactly a machine that
  wrote this without meaning to be funny.
- **Originality:** not a reuse of any line submitted before; written fresh
  for this competition.
